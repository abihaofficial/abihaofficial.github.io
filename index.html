How it usually goesFour stages
Listen

Ask too many questions

Leadership, staff, partners, funders. I look for the problem everyone already knows and nobody has written down.

Draft

Write something arguable

A short draft with clear choices: what we do, what we don't, who owns what. Then we fight about it, kindly.

Align

Get everyone saying the same words

Partners, funders and the team signed up to one version. Proposals and the first workplan go out.

Hand over

Make myself unnecessary

I stay through launch, train the people carrying it, and leave a system, not a slide deck.

What I'm thinking aboutOpen questions

What is a job, once AI does half of it?

I use AI every day. The interesting part is less the speed and more which parts of work stay human, and who decides.

Who owns the energy transition?

Households putting up their own panels are changing grids faster than many plans. I want programmes that start from them.

Is AI-made content honest?

I've built with AI content tools. I keep asking where the line sits between making a story clearer and making it up.

Can work look good?

Taste matters to me, in tailoring, in fragrance, in a well-made document. I think good design is a form of respect.

Me

I've spent most of my career at the start of things: first strategy hire, first communications hire, first person on a new workstream. That's where I'm happiest. I like the blank page, and I like the moment a plan stops being mine and becomes the team's.

I've worked inside think tanks, a diplomatic mission, global networks and a content studio, with colleagues on five continents. I'm curious about the future of work, the ethics of the tools we're all suddenly using, and how to make big transitions feel like something people own.
