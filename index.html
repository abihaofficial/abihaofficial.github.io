Hi, I'm Abiha. I like blank pages.

I'm usually the first person in the room when something new is being built: the first strategy hire, the first communications hire, the first name on a new workstream. I build the thing, find the people who should run it, and stay until it works without me.

These days I work independently from Lahore with climate, energy and development teams.

A few stories

Organisation names are left out on purpose. The work is real, and I'm happy to talk about any of it.

An energy think tank · Islamabad

The strategy nobody wanted to read

I joined as the first strategy hire. Everyone agreed the organisation needed a strategy, and nobody was looking forward to reading one.

So I started with conversations, not a template. I sat with leadership and every programme team, listened for the things people said in the corridor but never wrote down, and turned it into a plan short enough for the board to actually decide on. The team grew from fifteen to thirty-five while we did it.

Plain words are a form of respect.

A global clean energy network · Istanbul

Fifty people in one room

I was the first person on a new workstream inside a global network. It began as a concept note and a lot of good intentions.

Most of the real work happened before anyone booked a flight: months of calls and drafts with members in more than twenty countries, agreeing the words we could all stand behind. By the time fifty of us met in Istanbul, the room already knew what it was there to do.

A convening is mostly what happens before the room.

An AI content studio · Indonesia and Pakistan

Teaching creators, learning from them

I signed creators in two countries to tell clean energy stories, negotiated their rates, and wrote the contracts.

Then I designed a full training programme for creators in Indonesia: what to teach, in what order, and how. Writing it made me ask a question I still carry: when AI makes a story easier to tell, what keeps it honest?

The people closest to an audience usually know more than the people briefing them.

A foreign embassy · Islamabad

Writing in someone else's voice

For almost two years I wrote an Ambassador's speeches, talking points and press releases, and hosted national events as MC.

I learned to disappear into another person's voice, and to explain grant programmes and development finance to ministries, journalists and business groups who each needed something different from the same facts. I also grew the embassy's Twitter from zero.

Good writing for someone else sounds like them on their best day.

London Climate Action Week · RSA House

Three minutes

In June 2026 I gave a firestarter talk with exactly three minutes to say something worth remembering. I spent them on one idea: what changes when the people using the power also own it.

Short is harder than long, and worth it.

Working together

I'm most useful when something needs to exist and doesn't yet: a new programme, a strategy a board can act on, a coalition that needs to agree on its words, or a story that funders and the public will both understand. I listen first, write something people can argue with, and stay through launch.
